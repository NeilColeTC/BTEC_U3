# Week 10 Teacher Notes — Community Building and Social Media Policy

## Curriculum intent

The repository scaffold defines Week 10 as B4/B5: community-building techniques plus a business social-media policy covering professionalism, confidentiality, security, personal/business separation and legal/ethical considerations, with DojoLearn used to rehearse enquiries, complaints and other interactions.

## Connection to earlier learning

Week 5 introduced reputation and risk from an analytical perspective. Week 10 converts those ideas into **planned operational controls**. Learners should therefore recognise risks but now answer: **what will the campaign team actually do?**

## Community management

Avoid equating engagement with simply generating comments. Useful community management involves purposeful, sustainable interaction. A campaign that invites large volumes of interaction without resources or response processes may create a service/reputation problem.

## Moderation

Teach proportionality. Legitimate criticism should not automatically be removed. A defensible policy distinguishes complaints/criticism from spam, abuse, security threats and genuinely inappropriate content.

## Social-media policy

Policies should be operational. Require learners to move beyond slogans such as “be professional”, “keep data safe” or “use strong passwords”. A useful planning structure is:

**rule → reason → required action/escalation**.

## Legal/ethical considerations

Keep these connected to practical behaviour. Relevant areas may include privacy/data protection, copyright/permissions, confidentiality, misleading communication and respectful/non-discriminatory conduct. Do not imply that this teaching resource constitutes legal advice or require learners to reproduce legislation lists when the specification focus is policy practice.

## Security

Keep account-security planning proportionate and defensive: authorised access, secure authentication practices, suspicious-message awareness, reporting compromised accounts and avoiding accidental disclosure. Do not turn the lesson into offensive cybersecurity instruction.

## B.P3/B.M2 progression

For B.P3 preparation, learners should incorporate workable community/policy arrangements into the campaign plan. For B.M2, they should justify why selected approaches are appropriate given requirements, audience, risks and constraints.

Useful reasoning chain:

**decision → requirement/risk → likely benefit → limitation/trade-off → judgement**.

## Assessment boundary

Do not provide a ready-made policy for the learner's live assignment. Neutral examples, headings, questions and formative scenarios can teach the process while preserving learner ownership.

## DojoLearn evidence

Encourage learners to retain evidence of interaction decisions/responses and any simulator outcomes. This can later support implementation evidence and evaluation of campaign management.

## Preparation for Week 11

Learners should leave Week 10 with a campaign plan that addresses not only content publication but also what happens **after users interact**. Week 11 can then audit and refine the complete plan before implementation.
