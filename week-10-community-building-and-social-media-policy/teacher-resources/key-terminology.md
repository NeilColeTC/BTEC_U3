# Week 10 Key Terminology

| Term | Working definition | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Online community | Users who interact around a business, campaign or shared interest rather than only receiving content. | Community relationships can support engagement and loyalty. |
| Community management | Planned monitoring, interaction and response activity used to support an online community. | Campaign work continues after publishing. |
| Moderation | Applying defined rules to user-generated content/interactions. | Protects users/business while preserving legitimate participation. |
| Escalation | Passing an issue to someone with appropriate authority/expertise. | Prevents staff from mishandling sensitive or high-risk situations. |
| Social-media policy | Organisational rules/processes governing social-media use. | Supports consistency, professionalism, security and accountability. |
| Professionalism | Conduct appropriate to representing the business, including tone, accuracy and respectful communication. | Public responses affect trust/reputation. |
| Confidentiality | Protecting information that should not be disclosed to unauthorised people. | Social channels can expose customer/business information. |
| Sensitive information | Information requiring protection because disclosure could cause harm or breach obligations. | Should not be requested/exposed carelessly in public interactions. |
| Authorised user | Person permitted to access/manage a business social-media account. | Limits uncontrolled access and clarifies responsibility. |
| Personal/business separation | Keeping personal opinions/activity distinct from official organisational communication. | Reduces confusion and reputational risk. |
| Spam | Unwanted, irrelevant or repetitive content/activity. | May require moderation under policy. |
| Abuse | Threatening, harassing or seriously offensive interaction. | Requires a defined moderation/escalation response. |
| Complaint | Expression of dissatisfaction requiring appropriate acknowledgement/handling. | Can affect both customer outcome and wider reputation. |
| Constructive criticism | Negative feedback intended or capable of identifying a genuine issue/improvement. | Should not be confused automatically with abuse. |
| Incident record | Record of significant complaint, moderation, security or escalation action. | Supports accountability, consistency and review. |
| Proportionality | Matching the strength of a response/control to the seriousness of the issue. | Avoids excessive moderation or weak controls. |
