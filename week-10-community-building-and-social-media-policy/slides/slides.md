# Week 10 Slides — Community Building and Social Media Policy

## Slide 1 — Week 10
### Community Building and Social Media Policy
A campaign continues after the post is published.

---
## Slide 2 — Today's principle
**Interaction opportunity → appropriate response → policy boundary → escalation/recording → community/reputation outcome**

---
## Slide 3 — Audience vs community
An audience receives content. A community also interacts, contributes and develops an ongoing relationship with the business and other participants.

---
## Slide 4 — Community building
Purposeful, consistent interaction that encourages relevant participation and strengthens relationships around the business/campaign.

---
## Slide 5 — Broadcasting alone
Repeated one-way promotion may create visibility without meaningful relationships or conversation.

---
## Slide 6 — Techniques
Questions • useful replies • acknowledgement • follow-up information • appropriate sharing • consistent tone • professional complaint handling.

---
## Slide 7 — Response quality
A fast reply can still be poor. Consider accuracy, tone, usefulness, privacy and whether escalation is required.

---
## Slide 8 — Interaction triage
First classify what you are dealing with before choosing an action.

---
## Slide 9 — Possible categories
Enquiry • positive engagement • constructive criticism • complaint • misinformation • spam • abuse • security/confidentiality concern.

---
## Slide 10 — Respond?
Not every interaction needs the same response. Ask what outcome the action is intended to achieve.

---
## Slide 11 — Moderate?
Moderation should follow a defensible policy. Removing criticism simply because it is negative can damage trust.

---
## Slide 12 — Escalate?
Escalate when the issue exceeds the responder's authority, involves sensitive information, significant risk or requires specialist/business action.

---
## Slide 13 — Public or private?
Some issues benefit from a brief public acknowledgement followed by an appropriate private route. Avoid requesting or exposing sensitive information publicly.

---
## Slide 14 — Reputation
Other users may judge the business by how it responds, not only by the original complaint.

---
## Slide 15 — Why have a policy?
Consistency • professionalism • security • confidentiality • accountability • reputation • safer decision making.

---
## Slide 16 — Policy is operational
“Be professional” is too vague. Staff need to know what professional behaviour means in practice.

---
## Slide 17 — Good policy structure
**Rule → reason → required action/escalation**

---
## Slide 18 — Authorised use
Who may access business accounts? What permissions/responsibilities do they have?

---
## Slide 19 — Professional conduct
Tone, accuracy, respectful communication, corrections and appropriate representation of the organisation.

---
## Slide 20 — Confidentiality
Do not disclose customer, staff or business information that should remain private. Move sensitive conversations to an approved channel.

---
## Slide 21 — Security
Strong account-access practice, approved devices/processes, awareness of suspicious links/messages and reporting compromised access.

---
## Slide 22 — Personal vs business
Personal opinions/content should not accidentally be presented as official business communication.

---
## Slide 23 — Moderation rules
Define what may be moderated and why. Distinguish criticism from spam, abuse or genuinely harmful/inappropriate content.

---
## Slide 24 — Legal/ethical awareness
Policy should recognise relevant privacy/data protection, copyright/permissions, confidentiality, misleading communication and respectful/non-discriminatory conduct.

---
## Slide 25 — Do not turn policy into a law list
The important question is: **What should staff do differently because of the requirement/risk?**

---
## Slide 26 — Incident recording
Significant complaints, moderation actions, security incidents and escalations may need recording for accountability and review.

---
## Slide 27 — DojoLearn practical
Classify → decide → respond/action → policy reason → escalate if needed → consider wider audience.

---
## Slide 28 — B.P3
Community management and policy should form part of a workable campaign plan.

---
## Slide 29 — B.M2
Justify why selected controls/approaches are appropriate for this business, audience and campaign.

---
## Slide 30 — Trade-offs
Speed vs accuracy • openness vs privacy • freedom to respond vs approval control • engagement vs moderation workload.

---
## Slide 31 — Quality test
Could a new member of staff use your policy to make a consistent decision?

---
