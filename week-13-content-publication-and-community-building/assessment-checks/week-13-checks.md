# Week 13 — Assessment and Understanding Checks

## Criteria progression

**C.P5:** continue implementing the social-media campaign through planned publication and professional operation/community management.  
**BC.D3:** accumulate evidence and evaluative observations that can later support a justified assessment of campaign decisions and outcomes.

## Core checks

By the end of Week 13, learners should be able to:

- [ ] identify what campaign activity is live and what remains scheduled;
- [ ] publish content in line with the refined campaign plan;
- [ ] use varied content for a defined audience/campaign purpose;
- [ ] maintain campaign identity across platform-specific adaptations;
- [ ] use relevant visibility techniques where appropriate/available;
- [ ] explain why a hashtag/tag/share/following action is relevant;
- [ ] monitor audience interactions;
- [ ] distinguish enquiries, positive engagement, criticism, complaints, misinformation, spam, abuse and security/confidentiality concerns;
- [ ] explain why negative feedback is not automatically inappropriate;
- [ ] respond professionally within authority;
- [ ] moderate according to policy rather than personal preference;
- [ ] escalate issues appropriately;
- [ ] maintain privacy/confidentiality;
- [ ] update implementation evidence;
- [ ] maintain a community-management log;
- [ ] record early evaluative observations cautiously;
- [ ] identify evidence/metrics required for Week 14 analysis.

## Common misconceptions

- [ ] “Varied content means using every possible format.”
- [ ] “More hashtags always means more visibility.”
- [ ] “Tag as many accounts as possible.”
- [ ] “Any criticism damages the campaign and should be deleted.”
- [ ] “Community building means increasing follower numbers only.”
- [ ] “A fast response is always better than an accurate/escalated response.”
- [ ] “A single high-performing post proves the campaign worked.”
- [ ] “I only need evidence of posts, not interactions.”

## Evidence sources

Use published campaign content, implementation evidence log, visibility/community actions, community-management log, interaction evidence and evaluative observation log.

## Transition to analytics

Before Week 14, learners should be able to identify:

1. the baseline and campaign targets;
2. content/activity implemented to date;
3. available performance metrics;
4. significant interactions/community activity;
5. platform/content differences worth investigating;
6. deviations/limitations that may affect interpretation;
7. where all supporting evidence is stored.
