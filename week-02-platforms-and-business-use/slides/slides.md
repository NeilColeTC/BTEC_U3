# Week 2 Slides — Social Media Platforms and Business Use

## Slide 1 — Week 2
### Social Media Platforms and Business Use
Today: move from **what businesses do** to **how and why it helps**.

---
## Slide 2 — Retrieval
Define: business aim, target audience, brand, platform feature, engagement. Why is popularity alone not enough when choosing a platform?

---
## Slide 3 — A.P1 direction
A.P1 requires explanation of different ways businesses can use social media. Strong explanations connect **use → how → benefit → business context**.

---
## Slide 4 — Listing is not explaining
“A business can advertise.” is a list point. Ask: How? For what purpose? What could the business gain?

---
## Slide 5 — Creating an image or brand
Social media can help a business communicate a recognisable identity through consistent messages, visual presentation, tone and content.

---
## Slide 6 — Promoting products and services
Businesses can publish content that increases awareness, communicates features/benefits, announces launches or offers and directs audiences towards further action.

---
## Slide 7 — Communicating with customers
Communication can be two-way. Businesses can publish information and receive questions, comments and other responses.

---
## Slide 8 — Customer service
Social channels can support questions before purchase, help after purchase and provide updates. Consider speed, professionalism and public visibility.

---
## Slide 9 — Resolving queries and issues
A response can affect more than one customer when the interaction is visible. What opportunity and risk does this create?

---
## Slide 10 — Advertising
Advertising can extend reach beyond existing followers and can be aimed at selected audiences. It should still serve a clear business objective.

---
## Slide 11 — Social media is part of an ecosystem
Social profile ↔ website ↔ mobile app ↔ search/discovery ↔ customer action. A social-media post may be one stage in a wider customer journey.

---
## Slide 12 — Website and mobile links
Social content can direct users towards product information, booking, registration, purchasing or other business-controlled services.

---
## Slide 13 — Search-engine optimisation
An effective online presence aims to make useful business information/content discoverable. Social activity can form part of a wider strategy for online visibility; avoid treating social posting alone as a guaranteed ranking method.

---
## Slide 14 — Business profiles
A business profile provides an identifiable organisational presence and can communicate relevant business information while providing a base for publishing and interaction.

---
## Slide 15 — Usage data and analytics
Platforms can provide information about account/content use and interaction. Data becomes useful when it helps answer a business question or informs a decision.

---
## Slide 16 — DojoLearn feature audit
Investigate The Wall, The Pulse, The Grid, The Metronome, The Pin and The Tube. Which observed features support which business uses?

---
## Slide 17 — Objective before platform
Do not start with “Which platform do I like?” Start with: **What does the business need to achieve?** Then consider platform characteristics.

---
## Slide 18 — A.P1 practice
For each business use explain: 1) what the business does; 2) how social media enables it; 3) why that could benefit the business; 4) relevant context/example.

---
## Slide 19 — Improve this answer
“A café can use social media to promote food.” What is missing? Rewrite it as a developed explanation.

---
## Slide 20 — Exit ticket
Explain one business use; explain one role of websites/apps/profiles/analytics; justify one DojoLearn platform choice.
