# Week 1 Teacher Notes

## Curriculum intent

Week 1 should change the learner's perspective from **consumer/user** to **business communicator**.

Many learners will already use social media extensively. The teaching challenge is therefore not basic familiarity with posting and following but recognising that business use requires deliberate decisions about aims, audience, platform, content and eventual measures of success.

## Specification emphasis

Keep the principal specification focus on **A1 Social media websites**:

- platforms continually evolve;
- different platforms have different features, structures and target audiences;
- businesses can use social media to support aims and needs including branding, promotion, communication, customer service and query/issue management.

Do not attempt to complete all A1 content in Week 1. Advertising, website/mobile integration, SEO, business profiles, usage data and audience profiles receive fuller treatment in subsequent weeks.

## Assessment language

Introduce the assessment criteria but avoid turning Week 1 into assignment writing.

Useful learner distinction:

- **Explain** — make something clear by giving relevant detail/reasons.
- **Assess** — consider different factors/approaches and reach a supported judgement.
- **Evaluate** — weigh strengths, weaknesses and significance to reach a reasoned conclusion.

These command terms should be reinforced repeatedly as the unit progresses.

## DojoLearn teaching approach

The simulator should be treated as a learning environment rather than a software demonstration.

Avoid simply telling learners which real-world platform each simulated platform resembles. Ask instead:

- What does the interface encourage users to do?
- What content appears natural here?
- How might an audience interact?
- What business communication would fit this structure?
- What would not work well?

This develops transferable platform-analysis skills and avoids dependence on specific commercial services that may change over time.

## Suggested questioning

- What changes when you post on behalf of an organisation rather than yourself?
- Can the same post be appropriate personally but inappropriate professionally? Why?
- Why might a small business deliberately choose not to use a very popular platform?
- What would you need to know about a business before recommending a platform?
- How could a platform's structure influence the content a business produces?
- Is having many followers the same as achieving a business objective?

## Adaptive teaching

### Support

- pre-teach vocabulary;
- pair terms with concrete examples;
- provide sentence stems for justification;
- reduce the platform comparison to purpose/content/interaction before adding limitations;
- model one platform observation before independent investigation;
- allow paired exploration of DojoLearn while requiring individual written conclusions.

### Stretch

Ask learners to:

- identify circumstances where a platform would be unsuitable;
- compare two defensible platform choices;
- identify trade-offs rather than seeking a single “correct” platform;
- distinguish platform capability from business strategy;
- question unsupported assumptions about audiences.

## EDI

When discussing audiences, challenge stereotypes. Platform audiences should later be supported by research rather than assumptions such as “young people use X” or “older people use Y”. Introduce the principle now even though detailed demographic research occurs later.

## Evidence management

Week 1 outputs are formative. They may be retained in learner folders/portfolios as evidence of learning and reflection but should not be treated as completed Assignment 1 evidence.

## Preparation for Week 2

Review exit tickets before the next session. Week 2 should retrieve:

- business aim;
- target audience;
- brand;
- platform feature;
- business vs personal use;
- the principle that platform suitability depends on requirements.

Resolve any DojoLearn access issues before the practical work becomes more substantial.
