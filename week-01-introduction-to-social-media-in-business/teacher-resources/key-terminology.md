# Week 1 Key Terminology

| Term | Working definition | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Social media | Online platforms/services that enable users and organisations to create, publish, share and interact with content and other users. | Forms the environment businesses will plan for and use throughout the unit. |
| Social-media platform | A particular service/environment providing tools for publishing content and interacting with an audience. | Different platforms have different structures, features and audiences. |
| Business aim | A broad outcome a business wants to achieve. | Social-media activity should support purposeful business needs rather than exist simply because a platform is popular. |
| Business requirement | A specific need or condition that a proposed social-media solution should satisfy. | Becomes central to planning and evaluation in Learning Aims B and C. |
| Target audience | The group of people a business intends its communication, product or service to reach. | Platform and content decisions should be related to the intended audience. |
| Audience profile | Characteristics used to describe an audience, such as age, gender, interests or income. | Detailed audience profiling is required later for A.P2 and campaign planning. |
| Brand | The identity and image associated with a business, product or service. | Businesses can use social media to create and reinforce an image or brand. |
| Content | Material published through social media, such as text, images, video, links, polls or quizzes. | Content choice affects how a business communicates and engages audiences. |
| Promotion | Communication intended to increase awareness or interest in a business, product or service. | Promotion is one of the core business uses explored in Learning Aim A. |
| Customer service | Support provided to customers before, during or after a purchase/use of a service. | Social media can be used to answer questions, resolve queries and manage issues. |
| Engagement / interaction | Actions showing that users have interacted with content or an account. | Later campaign analysis will examine interactions with published content. |
| Follower | A user who chooses to receive or access updates from an account/profile. | Follower numbers can later form part of campaign targets and analytics. |
| Analytics | Data used to understand audience characteristics and the performance of social-media activity. | Learners later gather and interpret data to optimise campaign activity. |
| Platform feature | A function or capability offered by a social-media platform. | Businesses should match useful features to their communication needs and target audiences. |
| Profile | The business/account presence through which information and content are presented on a platform. | Learners later create and configure simulated business profiles. |
| Reputation | The way a business is perceived by customers and other stakeholders. | Social-media communication can strengthen or damage business reputation. |

## Teaching note

Treat these as working definitions for teaching rather than assignment-ready paragraphs. Encourage learners to apply each term in context and refine their understanding as the unit develops.
