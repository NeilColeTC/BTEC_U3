# Week 1 Lesson Plan — Introduction to Social Media in Business

**Duration:** 3 hours  
**Learning Aim:** A  
**Specification:** A1 Social media websites  
**Assessment preparation:** foundations for A.P1 and A.P2

## Learning outcomes

Learners will be able to:

- describe the structure of Unit 3 and its three learning aims;
- distinguish personal social-media use from purposeful business use;
- identify ways social media can support promotion and communication;
- compare social-media platform characteristics;
- navigate the DojoLearn simulation and identify the purposes of its six simulated platforms;
- use introductory Unit 3 terminology accurately.

## Prior knowledge

Learners are likely to have substantial experience as social-media users but may have limited understanding of social media as a planned business activity. Avoid assuming that frequent personal use automatically means strong understanding of business application.

## Resources

- Week 1 slides
- baseline quiz
- personal-versus-business activity
- platform investigation worksheet
- DojoLearn induction worksheet
- DojoLearn learner accounts/class access
- exit ticket

## Lesson sequence

### 1. Baseline and starter — 20 minutes

Learners complete the baseline quiz individually before discussion.

Use responses diagnostically to establish familiarity with:

- platform types;
- target audience;
- brand;
- engagement;
- business promotion;
- customer communication;
- analytics.

Do not turn the baseline into an assessed test. Capture misconceptions for later teaching.

**Check for understanding:** cold-call selected definitions and ask learners to distinguish an example of personal use from business use.

### 2. Unit induction — 25 minutes

Introduce the unit title and the three learning aims:

- A — explore the impact of social media on how businesses promote products and services;
- B — develop a plan to use social media in a business to meet requirements;
- C — implement the use of social media in a business.

Explain the overall journey:

**understand → plan → create → publish → interact → analyse → optimise → evaluate**

Explain that Assignment 1 addresses Learning Aim A and Assignment 2 addresses Learning Aims B and C. Keep discussion focused on expectations and evidence types rather than supplying assessment responses.

### 3. Teacher input — What changes when social media becomes business communication? — 35 minutes

Begin with familiar personal activities: posting photographs, messaging, following accounts, sharing content and commenting.

Develop these into purposeful business activities such as:

- creating a brand image;
- promoting products/services;
- communicating with customers;
- providing customer service;
- responding to queries and issues.

Emphasise intentionality: a business should be able to explain what it is trying to achieve, whom it is trying to reach and why a particular platform/content approach is appropriate.

### 4. Platform investigation — 35 minutes

Learners examine a range of social-media platform types/examples and compare:

- main purpose;
- structure;
- common content formats;
- communication style;
- likely audiences;
- possible business opportunities.

The objective is not to produce definitive demographic profiles in Week 1; detailed audience profiling is developed later.

**Adaptive support:** provide sentence starters such as “This platform could support a business by…” and “A limitation for this business might be…”.

**Stretch:** require learners to identify a business objective for which each platform would be unsuitable as well as suitable.

### Break — 10 minutes

### 5. DojoLearn induction — 45 minutes

Introduce the simulation as a controlled environment for practising business social-media decisions.

Learners access and investigate:

- The Wall
- The Pulse
- The Grid
- The Metronome
- The Pin
- The Tube

For each, learners record observations about content style, interaction model, likely communication strengths and potential business uses.

Avoid presenting a simplistic one-to-one “this is Facebook/Twitter/etc.” answer. Encourage learners to infer similarities and differences from platform characteristics.

**Teacher checks:** verify successful access, navigation and completion of observations. Ask learners to justify at least one platform/business match verbally.

### 6. Consolidation — 20 minutes

Give learners short business scenarios. They choose one or more simulated platforms and justify their choices.

Example scenarios:

- a local café announcing a new lunch menu;
- a charity explaining the impact of a fundraising project;
- a creative business showcasing visual work;
- a business needing to issue frequent short service updates.

Require decisions to refer to platform characteristics rather than preference.

### 7. Exit assessment — 10 minutes

Learners independently answer:

1. Give three different ways social media can support a business.
2. Explain one difference between personal and business use.
3. Choose one DojoLearn platform and explain one characteristic that could make it useful to a business.

Collect responses to inform Week 2 teaching.

## Assessment and checks for understanding

Evidence is formative:

- baseline quiz;
- questioning;
- platform comparison;
- DojoLearn investigation;
- scenario justification;
- exit ticket.

These activities prepare learners for later A.P1/A.P2 work but are not designed as summative assignment evidence.

## English, maths, EDI and employability

**English:** accurate use of vocabulary; concise explanation; adapting language between personal and professional contexts.  
**Maths:** introduce the idea that business decisions can later be evaluated through quantitative platform analytics; detailed analysis comes later.  
**EDI:** challenge assumptions that all demographic groups use platforms in identical ways and avoid stereotypical audience claims.  
**Employability:** professional digital communication, audience awareness, purposeful decision making and responsible use of online platforms.

## Reflection prompts for teacher

After the session note:

- Which terminology caused difficulty?
- Which learners treated personal experience as evidence of business suitability?
- Were learners able to justify platform choices rather than merely state preferences?
- Were there any DojoLearn access/usability issues to resolve before later practical sessions?
- What should be retrieved at the start of Week 2?
