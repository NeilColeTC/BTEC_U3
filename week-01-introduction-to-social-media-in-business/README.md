# Week 1 — Introduction to Social Media in Business

**Specification focus:** Learning Aim A — A1 Social media websites  
**Assessment preparation:** Foundation for A.P1 and A.P2; no summative assessment evidence intended this week.  
**Practical environment:** DojoLearn social-media simulation

## Week purpose

Week 1 establishes the conceptual and practical foundations for Unit 3. Learners are introduced to the three learning aims, the assessment journey and the distinction between personal and business use of social media. They begin to explore how social-media platforms have changed business promotion, communication and customer relationships.

The Pearson specification emphasises that social-media websites continually evolve, introduce new features and differ in structure, features and target audience. This week therefore focuses on recognising platform characteristics rather than memorising a fixed list of services.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the session learners should be able to:

1. Identify the three learning aims and describe the overall assessment journey for Unit 3.
2. Distinguish between personal and business uses of social media.
3. Explain at an introductory level how social media can support business promotion and communication.
4. Identify important characteristics that distinguish different types of social-media platform.
5. Navigate the DojoLearn simulation and recognise the purposes of The Wall, The Pulse, The Grid, The Metronome, The Pin and The Tube.
6. Begin using key Unit 3 terminology accurately.

## Specification links

### A1 Social media websites

Initial coverage includes:

- social-media websites continually evolving and introducing new features;
- features, structure and target audience of different social-media websites;
- introductory consideration of how businesses can use social media to support business aims and needs;
- creating an image or brand;
- promoting products and services;
- communicating with customers;
- customer service and resolving queries.

Detailed treatment of these business uses follows in Week 2.

## Session structure

The three-hour session combines unit induction, baseline assessment, explicit teaching, platform comparison and a guided DojoLearn practical.

| Phase | Approx. time | Focus |
|---|---:|---|
| Retrieval/baseline | 20 min | Existing knowledge and experience |
| Unit induction | 25 min | Learning aims and assessment journey |
| Teacher input | 35 min | Personal vs business social media and changing business communication |
| Platform investigation | 35 min | Features, structure, purpose and audience |
| Break | 10 min | — |
| DojoLearn induction | 45 min | Guided exploration of simulated platforms |
| Consolidation activity | 20 min | Business-use/platform matching |
| Exit assessment | 10 min | Three ways social media can support a business |

## DojoLearn simulation

Learners explore:

- **The Wall**
- **The Pulse**
- **The Grid**
- **The Metronome**
- **The Pin**
- **The Tube**

The purpose is not simply to learn the interface. Learners investigate how platform design influences the type of content that can be communicated, the likely audience experience and potential business uses.

Learners record observations using the simulation platform investigation worksheet.

## Adaptive teaching

Support is provided through explicit vocabulary teaching, worked examples, platform feature prompts and structured comparison tables. Learners who need additional scaffolding can focus initially on purpose, content type and audience before considering more subtle platform differences.

Stretch learners should explain *why* a particular platform characteristic could make it more or less appropriate for a particular business objective and identify possible limitations of relying on one platform.

## Assessment boundary

Week 1 activities are formative. They build the vocabulary, concepts and platform awareness required for later assessment but should not become a completed response to Assignment 1. Learners should make their own decisions and use independently selected real-life examples when completing assessed work.
