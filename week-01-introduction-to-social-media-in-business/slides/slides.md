## Slide 6 — Personal or business?

Consider these activities:

- uploading a holiday photograph;
- announcing a new product;
- messaging a friend;
- responding to a customer complaint;
- sharing a meme;
- publishing opening-time changes;
- following a celebrity;
- answering a customer's product question.

Which are personal? Which are business? Could context change your answer?

---

## Slide 7 — Purpose changes everything

A business should ask:

- **What are we trying to achieve?**
- **Who are we trying to reach?**
- **What should we communicate?**
- **Which platform is appropriate?**
- **How will we know whether it worked?**

Social media for business is purposeful communication, not simply “posting”.

---

## Slide 8 — Introductory business uses

Businesses can use social media to support aims and needs including:

- creating an image or brand;
- promoting products and services;
- communicating with customers;
- customer service;
- resolving queries and managing issues.

We will explore these in much greater depth next week.

---

## Slide 9 — Social-media platforms evolve

Platforms are not static.

They introduce new:

- features;
- content formats;
- advertising tools;
- interaction methods;
- business tools.

This means businesses need to understand **characteristics**, not simply memorise platform names.

---

## Slide 10 — What can make platforms different?

Compare:

- structure;
- target audience;
- content formats;
- length/style of communication;
- public vs more targeted interaction;
- discoverability;
- community features;
- visual/video/text emphasis.

---

## Slide 11 — Platform investigation

For each platform/example consider:

1. What appears to be its main purpose?
2. What content works naturally there?
3. How do users interact?
4. What might make it useful to a business?
5. What might limit its usefulness?

---

## Slide 12 — Don't confuse popularity with suitability

A large platform is not automatically the best platform for every business.

**Question:** What information would you need before recommending a platform to a business?

## Slide 14 — Your investigation challenge

Explore each simulated platform.

Record:

- content style;
- interaction features;
- communication strengths;
- possible business uses;
- possible limitations.

Do not just ask “Which real platform is this copying?”

Ask: **What kind of communication does its design encourage?**

## Slide 15 — Business scenario challenge

Your business has an objective.

Choose a simulated platform and justify the choice using its characteristics.

A strong answer uses:

**business objective + audience/communication need + platform characteristic + reason**

## Slide 16 — Example reasoning structure
