# Week 16 Teacher Notes — Campaign Evaluation

## Curriculum intent

The repository scaffold defines Week 16 as C5 and combined B/C evaluation: evaluate the plan and implemented campaign against business requirements, success criteria and performance data, considering legal/ethical implications and professional behaviours. DojoLearn supplies accumulated campaign evidence and analytics. Assessment focus is BC.D2/BC.D3.

## Central distinction

Learners frequently produce description or analysis when the criterion requires evaluation.

Use:

- description = what happened;
- analysis = relationships/patterns and what they may indicate;
- evaluation = supported judgement of effectiveness/value against a criterion.

## Evaluation framework

Require:

**criterion → evidence → comparison → judgement → significance → limitation → recommendation**.

The significance stage is especially useful for moving learners beyond “this was good/bad”.

## Evaluating the plan

Implementation evidence can legitimately be used to evaluate the original planning decisions. For example, a vague success criterion may only reveal its practical weakness once the learner attempts to judge campaign performance.

## Evaluating the campaign

Encourage mixed judgements. A campaign may improve reach while missing an action target, reach the correct audience but generate weak interaction, or improve after optimisation without enough observation time to establish a robust conclusion.

## Legal/ethical/professional implications

Avoid a generic law list. Learners should evaluate issues actually relevant to their campaign and explain consequences, management and effectiveness.

## Recommendations

Generic statements such as “post more” are weak. Recommendations should emerge from evidenced problems/opportunities, be prioritised and include a way to measure future success.

## BC.D2 / BC.D3 boundary

Keep the learner's own judgement central. The supplied matrices structure reasoning and evidence location but deliberately do not contain personalised completed paragraphs.

## Assessment authenticity

Do not retrospectively invent missing targets, rationales or campaign evidence. Where evidence is absent, the learner can identify that as a limitation and explain the consequence.

## Preparation for Week 17

Week 17 should focus on completeness, cross-referencing, criterion mapping, evidence quality, presentation and final quality assurance rather than introducing new campaign decisions.
