# Week 16 Key Terminology

| Term | Working definition | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Evaluation | Reaching a supported judgement about value, quality or effectiveness against relevant criteria. | Central to BC.D2/BC.D3. |
| Fitness for purpose | Extent to which a plan/solution is suitable for its intended business requirements and objectives. | Key basis for judging the campaign plan. |
| Evaluation criterion | Standard/question against which evidence is judged. | Prevents evaluation becoming personal opinion. |
| Success criterion | Defined measure/condition used to judge achievement of an objective. | Provides an explicit benchmark. |
| Synthesis | Combining evidence from different sources/stages into a coherent judgement. | Required for whole-campaign evaluation. |
| Traceability | Ability to connect a claim/judgement to requirements, decisions and supporting evidence. | Strengthens reliability and assessment evidence. |
| Significance | Importance/consequence of a finding to the business, audience or objective. | Moves writing beyond simple strengths/weaknesses. |
| Contradictory evidence | Evidence that challenges or weakens an initial judgement. | Supports balanced evaluation. |
| Limitation | Factor restricting the confidence or scope of a conclusion. | Essential for credible judgement. |
| Recommendation | Evidence-based proposal for future improvement/action. | Shows how evaluation informs future practice. |
| Priority | Relative importance/urgency assigned to an action. | Helps distinguish strategic recommendations from a generic list. |
| Legal implication | Consequence arising from relevant law/legal responsibility. | Campaign decisions must account for applicable responsibilities. |
| Ethical implication | Consequence related to fairness, honesty, privacy, inclusion or responsible conduct. | Legal compliance alone does not guarantee responsible practice. |
| Professional behaviour | Conduct expected in a business context, including accuracy, reliability, communication and responsibility. | Influences campaign quality and reputation. |
| Trade-off | Situation where improving one outcome may reduce another. | Supports nuanced evaluation. |
| Confidence | Degree of certainty justified by available evidence. | Helps qualify conclusions appropriately. |
| Counterfactual | Consideration of what might reasonably have happened under an alternative decision. | Can deepen evaluation when used cautiously, without inventing evidence. |
