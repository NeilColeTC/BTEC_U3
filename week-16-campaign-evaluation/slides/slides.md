# Week 16 Slides — Campaign Evaluation

## Slide 1 — Week 16
### Campaign Evaluation
Judge the campaign using the evidence you created.

---
## Slide 2 — C5 and B/C evaluation
Evaluate the plan and implemented campaign against requirements, success criteria and performance evidence.

---
## Slide 3 — Evidence chain
**Requirements → plan → refinement → implementation → interaction → analytics → optimisation → evaluation**

---
## Slide 4 — Description
What happened?

---
## Slide 5 — Analysis
What relationships/patterns does the evidence show and what might they mean?

---
## Slide 6 — Evaluation
How effective/appropriate was it **against a criterion**, and how strong is the evidence for that judgement?

---
## Slide 7 — Evaluation cycle
**Criterion/question → evidence → comparison → judgement → significance → limitation → recommendation**

---
## Slide 8 — Start with criteria
Business requirements and success criteria prevent evaluation becoming personal opinion.

---
## Slide 9 — Evaluate the plan
Was it fit for purpose before implementation?

---
## Slide 10 — Requirements coverage
Did the plan address what the business/client actually required?

---
## Slide 11 — Objectives and success criteria
Were objectives measurable and useful for later judgement?

---
## Slide 12 — Audience planning
Was the target audience sufficiently understood to guide decisions?

---
## Slide 13 — Platform selection
Were platforms justified against audience, purpose, content and business needs?

---
## Slide 14 — Content strategy
Did content choices support campaign objectives and audience needs?

---
## Slide 15 — Publishing plan
Were timing, frequency, resources and sequencing workable?

---
## Slide 16 — Policy and risk planning
Did the plan prepare the campaign for interaction, moderation, legal/ethical issues and reputation risks?

---
## Slide 17 — Review and refinement
What changed before implementation, why, and did the refinement improve fitness for purpose?

---
## Slide 18 — Evaluate implementation
Did the implemented campaign follow the plan where appropriate?

---
## Slide 19 — Deviations
A justified deviation can be stronger than blindly following a flawed plan.

---
## Slide 20 — Performance evidence
Use analytics, interactions and success criteria — not impressions alone.

---
## Slide 21 — Baseline and target
What changed from the starting position and how close was the intended outcome?

---
## Slide 22 — Content performance
Which content performed strongly/weakly against relevant objectives and why might that matter?

---
## Slide 23 — Audience fit
How well did actual audience evidence match the intended audience?

---
## Slide 24 — Community management
How effectively were enquiries, criticism, complaints and other interactions handled?

---
## Slide 25 — Optimisation
What did analytics cause you to change and what did subsequent evidence indicate?

---
## Slide 26 — Mixed evidence
Campaigns can succeed against one objective and underperform against another.

---
## Slide 27 — Balance
Strong evaluation acknowledges both evidence supporting and challenging the judgement.

---
## Slide 28 — Significance
Explain **why the finding matters to the business/objective/audience**.

---
## Slide 29 — Legal implications
Evaluate how relevant legal responsibilities affected decisions and risk.

---
## Slide 30 — Ethical implications
Consider fairness, honesty, privacy, inclusion and responsible communication in context.

---
## Slide 31 — Accessibility and inclusion
A campaign should not improve one metric by making communication less accessible or excluding the intended audience.

---
## Slide 32 — Reputation
How did campaign decisions protect or expose the organisation's reputation?

---
## Slide 33 — Professional behaviours
Planning, accuracy, communication, responsiveness, evidence management and willingness to act on data all affect campaign quality.

---
## Slide 34 — Limitations
What prevents you being completely confident in your conclusion?

---
## Slide 35 — Examples of limitations
Simulation conditions • sample size • observation period • metric availability • unequal exposure • confounding variables • audience-data limits.

---
## Slide 36 — Recommendations
Recommendations should solve evidenced problems or exploit evidenced opportunities.

---
## Slide 37 — Recommendation structure
**Evidence/problem → improvement → expected benefit → objective/requirement → priority → resource/limitation**

---
## Slide 38 — Prioritise
Not every possible improvement is equally valuable.

---
## Slide 39 — BC.D2
Evaluate the plan against business requirements and justify its fitness for purpose using evidence from planning/review.

---
## Slide 40 — BC.D3
Evaluate the implemented campaign using performance evidence, optimisation, legal/ethical/professional implications and justified recommendations.

---
## Slide 41 — Traceability
A reader should be able to follow every important judgement back to evidence.

---
## Slide 42 — Avoid retrospective invention
Do not create targets, reasons or evidence now that did not exist during the campaign.

---
## Slide 43 — Final test
**What is your judgement, what proves it, why does it matter, and what would you do next?**

---
## Slide 44 — Week 17
Complete, cross-reference and quality-assure the assessment evidence.
