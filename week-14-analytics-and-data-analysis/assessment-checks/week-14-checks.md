# Week 14 — Assessment and Understanding Checks

## Criteria progression

**C.P6:** gather and analyse campaign data in line with C4, including individual-content interaction, intended-versus-actual audience evidence and identification of high-/low-performing content.

Week 14 analysis also supplies the evidence needed for Week 15 optimisation and Week 16 C.M3/BC.D3 evaluation.

## Core checks

By the end of Week 14, learners should be able to:

- [ ] identify relevant campaign analysis questions;
- [ ] collect available DojoLearn analytics accurately;
- [ ] preserve analytics evidence;
- [ ] distinguish missing data from zero;
- [ ] distinguish raw counts from rates;
- [ ] identify an appropriate denominator where a rate is used;
- [ ] calculate percentage change correctly where valid;
- [ ] compare baseline/current/target where supported;
- [ ] analyse interaction with individual content/posts;
- [ ] compare content fairly rather than using one raw total automatically;
- [ ] identify relatively high-performing content with evidence;
- [ ] identify relatively low-performing content with evidence;
- [ ] compare intended and actual audiences using available evidence;
- [ ] use “insufficient evidence” rather than infer unsupported audience characteristics;
- [ ] identify patterns and anomalies;
- [ ] distinguish correlation from causation;
- [ ] state limitations/alternative explanations;
- [ ] create traceable analytical findings;
- [ ] generate evidence-based optimisation questions for Week 15.

## Common misconceptions

- [ ] “Copying analytics numbers is analysis.”
- [ ] “The biggest number is always the best result.”
- [ ] “Likes are the most important metric for every objective.”
- [ ] “Missing data means zero.”
- [ ] “Two percentages are comparable even when calculated from different definitions.”
- [ ] “The post with the most interactions must have the highest engagement rate.”
- [ ] “I can infer audience demographics from profile appearance.”
- [ ] “If a high-performing post used video, video caused the performance.”
- [ ] “An anomaly should be ignored.”

## Evidence sources

Use DojoLearn analytics evidence, campaign data-capture table, calculations, individual-post analysis, intended/actual audience comparison, analytics interpretation log and links to implementation/community evidence.

## Transition to optimisation

Before Week 15, learners should have:

1. a reliable campaign dataset/evidence pack;
2. identified high- and low-performing content;
3. intended-versus-actual audience findings;
4. baseline/target comparisons where available;
5. important patterns/anomalies;
6. stated limitations;
7. specific optimisation questions supported by evidence.
