# Week 14 Lesson Plan — Analytics and Data Analysis

**Duration:** 3 hours  
**Learning Aim:** C  
**Specification:** C4  
**Criteria preparation:** C.P6

## Learning outcomes

Learners will extract, organise, compare and interpret DojoLearn campaign analytics and use the evidence to identify content/audience performance patterns.

## Lesson sequence

### 1. Retrieval and evidence inventory — 15 minutes

Retrieve baseline, target, implementation evidence, community-management evidence, engagement, reach/visibility concepts and the Week 13 evaluative-observation framework.

Learners identify what evidence already exists and what analytics must now be collected.

### 2. Data is not analysis — 20 minutes

Contrast:

- **data:** recorded values;
- **comparison:** relationship between values;
- **interpretation:** what the relationship may indicate;
- **judgement:** supported conclusion in relation to a question/objective.

Introduce:

**question → metric/evidence → comparison → interpretation → limitation → conclusion**.

### 3. Metrics and fair comparisons — 25 minutes

Review metrics available in DojoLearn. Where data permits, teach derived measures such as percentage change and interaction/engagement rates. Emphasise denominator choice and that raw totals can mislead when exposure differs.

### 4. Worked neutral dataset — 20 minutes

Learners calculate and compare a small fictional dataset, identify the apparent strongest/weakest content and then reconsider after exposure/audience context is revealed.

### 5. Intended vs actual audience — 20 minutes

Revisit the Week 7 target audience. Compare intended audience characteristics with the audience evidence actually available. Distinguish match, partial match, mismatch and unavailable evidence.

### Break — 10 minutes

### 6. DojoLearn analytics workshop — 55 minutes

Learners extract campaign data, preserve evidence snapshots, populate the analytics data-capture table and compare content/platform/audience performance.

### 7. Interpretation workshop — 20 minutes

Learners identify high/low performers, patterns and anomalies using:

**claim → evidence → comparison → possible explanation → limitation → implication**.

Avoid claiming that correlation proves a particular content feature caused performance.

### 8. Exit ticket — 15 minutes

Learners identify one defensible finding, one misleading metric/comparison they avoided and one optimisation question for Week 15.

## Adaptive teaching

**Support:** metric glossary, calculation templates, worked examples, sentence structures and preformatted analysis tables.  
**Stretch:** normalised comparisons, conflicting metrics, outliers, sample-size limitations, platform comparability and alternative explanations.

## English, maths, EDI and employability

**English:** evidence-led analytical writing and qualified conclusions.  
**Maths:** totals, differences, percentage change, rates, proportions and comparison of datasets.  
**EDI:** avoid assuming audience characteristics that analytics do not demonstrate; recognise limits of demographic categorisation/data.  
**Employability:** analytics reporting, KPI interpretation, data literacy, evidence management and decision support.

## Teacher reflection

- Are learners analysing rather than copying dashboard numbers?
- Are comparisons fair?
- Can learners explain denominators/rates where used?
- Are intended/actual audience comparisons evidence-based?
- Are learners distinguishing correlation from causation?
- Does each conclusion identify a limitation?
