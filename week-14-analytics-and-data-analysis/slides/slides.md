# Week 14 Slides — Analytics and Data Analysis

## Slide 1 — Week 14
### Analytics and Data Analysis
Turn campaign activity into evidence.

---
## Slide 2 — C4
Gather and interpret campaign data, analyse individual posts, compare intended and actual audiences and identify high/low performers.

---
## Slide 3 — Analytics cycle
**Question → select metric/evidence → collect → organise → compare → interpret → qualify → conclude**

---
## Slide 4 — Data ≠ analysis
A table of numbers tells us what was recorded. Analysis explains relevant relationships and what they may mean.

---
## Slide 5 — Four levels
**Data → comparison → interpretation → judgement**

---
## Slide 6 — Start with the question
Which campaign objective, audience question or performance issue are you trying to investigate?

---
## Slide 7 — Select relevant evidence
Not every available metric is relevant to every objective.

---
## Slide 8 — Vanity metrics
A large number can look impressive without demonstrating meaningful progress towards the objective.

---
## Slide 9 — Raw counts
Likes, comments, shares, clicks, views/reach or other available totals describe volume.

---
## Slide 10 — Context matters
A post with 50 interactions from 1,000 exposures is not directly equivalent to 40 interactions from 200 exposures.

---
## Slide 11 — Rates
Rates relate an outcome to an appropriate denominator and can support fairer comparisons.

---
## Slide 12 — Generic rate structure
**rate = relevant actions ÷ relevant opportunities × 100**

---
## Slide 13 — Percentage change
**(new − original) ÷ original × 100**
Use only when the original value is suitable and non-zero.

---
## Slide 14 — Denominator discipline
Always state what your rate is a percentage **of**.

---
## Slide 15 — DojoLearn metrics
Use the metrics the simulation actually provides. Do not invent missing analytics.

---
## Slide 16 — Individual-post analysis
Compare content using relevant measures and context rather than ranking by one number automatically.

---
## Slide 17 — High performer
A content item that performs strongly against a relevant comparison/objective — not simply the item with the largest visible number.

---
## Slide 18 — Low performer
A weaker result can still provide useful evidence about audience, format, message, timing or strategy.

---
## Slide 19 — Compare like with like
Consider exposure, platform, timing, audience opportunity, format and campaign purpose.

---
## Slide 20 — Multiple metrics
Reach may be strong while interaction is weak. Click/action may be strong even when likes are modest.

---
## Slide 21 — Patterns
Repeated relationships across several observations are generally more informative than one isolated result.

---
## Slide 22 — Anomalies
An unusual result deserves investigation, not automatic exclusion or over-generalisation.

---
## Slide 23 — Intended audience
Return to the audience defined during campaign planning.

---
## Slide 24 — Actual audience
Use only audience evidence the simulator/data actually supports.

---
## Slide 25 — Audience comparison
**Match • partial match • mismatch • insufficient evidence**.

---
## Slide 26 — Do not infer demographics
If the data does not show an audience characteristic, do not claim it does.

---
## Slide 27 — Baseline
What was the measured starting state before implementation?

---
## Slide 28 — Target
What outcome did the campaign intend to reach?

---
## Slide 29 — Progress
Compare baseline/current/target using relevant evidence where available.

---
## Slide 30 — Claim and evidence
Every analytical claim should point to a specific metric, comparison or interaction record.

---
## Slide 31 — Correlation
Two things occurring together can suggest a relationship worth investigating.

---
## Slide 32 — Causation
Campaign analytics alone may not prove that one feature directly caused the result.

---
## Slide 33 — Better wording
Use **may indicate**, **is consistent with**, **suggests**, or identify alternative explanations where certainty is limited.

---
## Slide 34 — Analysis structure
**Claim → evidence → comparison → possible explanation → limitation → implication**

---
## Slide 35 — C.P6
Evidence should show that campaign data was gathered and used to analyse performance, including content interaction and audience evidence required by C4.

---
## Slide 36 — Evidence pack
Preserve dashboard/data evidence, calculations, analysis tables and references to implementation/community evidence.

---
## Slide 37 — Week 15 bridge
Analytics should produce questions and decisions for optimisation — not merely a report filed away.

---
## Slide 38 — Exit question
**What should change because of what the evidence shows?**
