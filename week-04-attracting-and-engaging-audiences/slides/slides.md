# Week 4 Slides — Attracting and Engaging Audiences

## Slide 1 — Week 4
### Attracting and Engaging Audiences
Today we combine **business purpose + audience + content + platform**.

---
## Slide 2 — Retrieval
What is a target audience? What makes an audience profile useful? Give three business uses of social media. Why must audience claims be evidence based?

---
## Slide 3 — A.M1
A.M1 asks you to **assess** different ways a business can use social media to attract a target audience.

---
## Slide 4 — Explain vs assess
**Explain:** how and why.  
**Assess:** strengths + limitations + context + comparison + supported judgement.

---
## Slide 5 — No universally best content
The effectiveness of content depends on: objective, audience, platform, message, resources and context.

---
## Slide 6 — Text
Useful for information, updates, explanation and conversation. Consider clarity, length, tone and platform conventions.

---
## Slide 7 — Images
Can communicate visually and attract attention quickly. Consider quality, branding, accessibility and whether the message needs explanation.

---
## Slide 8 — Video
Can demonstrate, explain, entertain or tell a story. Consider production effort, viewing context, duration and accessibility.

---
## Slide 9 — Links
Can move users towards detailed information, booking, registration or purchasing. A click is a step in a customer journey, not automatically a successful outcome.

---
## Slide 10 — Polls and quizzes
Can invite active participation and generate interaction or insight. Ask whether the interaction supports the business objective.

---
## Slide 11 — Content purpose
Content may provide information, promote, entertain/use humour, present offers or support customers. One post can serve more than one purpose.

---
## Slide 12 — Humour
Humour may increase attention or relatability for some audiences but can be misunderstood, inappropriate or inconsistent with the brand.

---
## Slide 13 — Offers
Offers can create a reason to act but repeated discounting may not always support longer-term brand objectives. Context matters.

---
## Slide 14 — Keywords
Appropriate words/phrases can help content communicate relevance and support discovery. Keyword choice should reflect the intended audience and subject.

---
## Slide 15 — Hashtags and tags
Where platform features support them, tags can help organise/discover content or connect it with conversations. Relevance matters more than simply adding many tags.

---
## Slide 16 — Networking and sharing
Interaction with users, organisations and communities can extend visibility and strengthen relationships. Businesses still need professional, purposeful participation.

---
## Slide 17 — Advertising
Paid promotion can extend or target reach, but cost alone does not guarantee a useful business outcome.

---
## Slide 18 — Reach ≠ engagement ≠ outcome
**Reach:** who could see it.  
**Engagement:** who interacts.  
**Outcome:** what useful result follows for the business.

---
## Slide 19 — DojoLearn challenge
Create three alternative post concepts for one objective and persona. Change content format, message, engagement technique or platform.

---
## Slide 20 — Compare alternatives
For each ask: What is the strength? What is the limitation? Why does that matter for this audience/objective?

---
## Slide 21 — Judgement language
“Overall…” “For this audience…” “Although…” “This is likely to be more suitable because…” “The main limitation is…”

---
## Slide 22 — Avoid unsupported certainty
Say **likely**, **may**, **could**, supported by evidence/reasoning—not “will definitely go viral”.

---
## Slide 23 — A.M1 practice
Compare different social-media approaches for a neutral fictional business. Reach a supported conclusion.

---
## Slide 24 — Exit ticket
What turns an explanation into an assessment? Make one comparative judgement.
