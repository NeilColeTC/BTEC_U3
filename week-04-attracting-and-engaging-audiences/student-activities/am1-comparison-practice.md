# A.M1 Formative Practice — Assessing Ways to Attract a Target Audience

**This activity develops assessment skills. It is not Assignment 1 evidence.**

## Scenario

A fictional independent science centre wants to increase attendance at weekend family events. Its intended audience is adults responsible for choosing family leisure activities, particularly those interested in educational experiences for children.

The centre is considering several approaches:

- visually led posts showing activities;
- short videos demonstrating experiments;
- informational posts linking to booking information;
- polls/quizzes designed to encourage interaction;
- paid promotion aimed at a relevant audience.

## Task

Assess **at least three** different ways the science centre could use social media to attract its target audience.

Your response should:

- explain how each approach could attract the audience;
- relate the approach to relevant audience needs/interests;
- identify strengths;
- identify limitations or conditions affecting success;
- compare the approaches rather than treating them independently;
- reach a supported judgement about which approach or combination should be prioritised.

## Assessment-writing frame

You may structure each comparison using:

**Approach → audience relevance → strength → limitation → comparison → significance**

Finish with:

**Overall judgement → strongest reason → important limitation/condition**

## Peer-review checklist

- [ ] More than one approach is assessed.
- [ ] The target audience is central to the reasoning.
- [ ] Strengths and limitations are contextualised.
- [ ] Approaches are explicitly compared.
- [ ] The conclusion follows from the preceding reasoning.
- [ ] Claims avoid unsupported certainty.

## Stretch

Explain why the best strategy may combine approaches rather than selecting only one.
