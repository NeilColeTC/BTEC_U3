# Week 4 Teacher Notes

## Curriculum intent

Week 4 is the deliberate bridge from Pass-level explanation into Merit-level assessment. Learners already know different business uses and audience-profile concepts. The new intellectual demand is to compare how effectively different approaches may attract a particular target audience.

## Specification fidelity

Keep the practical/content work within A2 areas established in the course map:

- text, image, video, links, polls and quizzes;
- information, promotion, humour, offers and customer service;
- engaging audiences;
- keywords;
- networking;
- sharing;
- advertising;
- links with company/e-commerce websites.

## Teaching A.M1

Do not teach “add one advantage and one disadvantage” as a mechanical Merit formula. Assessment requires significance and judgement.

A useful progression is:

1. What is the business trying to achieve?
2. Who is the target audience?
3. How could approach A attract them?
4. What strengthens or limits that approach?
5. How does approach B differ?
6. Which difference matters most in this context?
7. What judgement follows?

## Reach, engagement and outcome

Learners often treat likes or views as success. Introduce the distinction now so later analytics work has a strong conceptual foundation. A large reach may produce little interaction; high interaction may still fail to produce the intended business action.

## Content-format neutrality

Avoid rules such as “video is best” or “young audiences prefer X”. Encourage conditional language and evidence. Format suitability depends on message, audience, objective, platform and resources.

## Humour and EDI

Humour is explicitly part of A2 but needs careful treatment. Discuss tone, cultural interpretation, accessibility and brand fit. This creates a natural EDI link without turning Week 4 into the risk/reputation lesson scheduled for Week 5.

## DojoLearn

The practical should generate alternatives. Learners need something concrete to compare, so do not let everyone create one post and stop. Require at least three variations and a written recommendation.

Do not claim the simulated platforms possess real-world demographic profiles unless the simulator itself provides evidence. Learners can use the interface/feature characteristics to judge format suitability while audience evidence comes from appropriate research or the supplied persona.

## Adaptive teaching

### Support
- supply the business objective/persona;
- reduce choice to two content formats before introducing three;
- use strength/limitation prompts;
- provide comparison connectives;
- orally rehearse judgement before writing.

### Stretch
- require a combination strategy;
- identify trade-offs;
- distinguish short-term engagement from business outcomes;
- propose evidence that would test the judgement;
- write conditional conclusions rather than absolute claims.

## Assessment boundary

The science-centre scenario and DojoLearn practice are deliberately neutral. Learners should independently research and apply reasoning to the live assignment context.

## Preparation for Week 5

Week 5 introduces A3 risks/issues and develops A.D1 evaluation. Retrieve the idea that an approach can simultaneously create an opportunity and a risk.
