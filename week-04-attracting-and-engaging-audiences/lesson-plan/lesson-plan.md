# Week 4 Lesson Plan — Attracting and Engaging Audiences

**Duration:** 3 hours  
**Learning Aim:** A  
**Specification:** A2  
**Primary criterion preparation:** A.M1

## Learning outcomes

Learners will compare content formats and audience-attraction techniques, apply them to target audiences and make supported judgements about likely suitability.

## Lesson sequence

### 1. Retrieval — 15 minutes

Retrieve Week 2 and Week 3 concepts: business uses, target audience, audience profile, platform characteristics, analytics and evidence-based audience decisions.

### 2. Command-term transition: explain to assess — 20 minutes

Model the difference:

- **Explain:** show how/why an approach could work.
- **Assess:** consider relevant strengths, limitations and context, compare approaches and reach a supported judgement.

Use a simple question such as whether image or video content would be more appropriate for a fictional product launch. Demonstrate that the answer depends on objective, audience, platform and message.

### 3. Content formats and purposes — 35 minutes

Teach and compare:

- text;
- image;
- video;
- links;
- polls;
- quizzes;
- information;
- promotion;
- humour;
- offers;
- customer-service content.

Learners complete the content-format comparison activity.

### 4. Attracting and extending reach — 30 minutes

Introduce and connect:

- appropriate keywords;
- hashtags/tags where supported;
- networking and community interaction;
- sharing/reposting;
- advertising;
- links to company/e-commerce websites.

Learners complete the engagement-techniques activity, distinguishing **reach**, **engagement** and **business outcome**.

### Break — 10 minutes

### 5. DojoLearn persona post challenge — 45 minutes

Provide a fictional business objective and target-audience persona. Learners create at least three alternative post concepts using different content/platform approaches in the simulation.

For each alternative they record:

- intended platform;
- content format;
- message/purpose;
- audience adaptation;
- attraction/engagement technique;
- expected strength;
- limitation.

Learners compare the alternatives and recommend one.

### 6. A.M1 formative comparison — 35 minutes

Learners independently complete the A.M1 practice task. Require a comparative judgement rather than separate descriptions.

Peer review against the assessment reasoning checklist.

### 7. Exit ticket — 10 minutes

Learners explain the difference between explanation and assessment and make one short supported judgement about an audience-attraction approach.

## Adaptive teaching

**Support:** comparison grids, audience/objective prompts, sentence stems and pre-selected alternatives.  
**Stretch:** require conditional judgements, trade-offs, multi-platform approaches and recognition that effectiveness depends on evidence rather than certainty.

## English, maths, EDI and employability

**English:** comparative language, causal reasoning and judgement vocabulary.  
**Maths:** interpreting relative audience/performance measures conceptually when comparing approaches.  
**EDI:** humour, imagery, language and assumptions can include or exclude audiences; encourage accessible and culturally aware choices.  
**Employability:** digital marketing, audience adaptation, creative decision making and evidence-based recommendation.

## Teacher reflection

- Are learners genuinely comparing rather than writing two separate explanations?
- Do judgements refer to audience and objective?
- Can learners identify limitations without concluding that an approach is useless?
- Are DojoLearn decisions based on platform characteristics rather than real-platform assumptions?
