# Week 11 Slides — Reviewing and Refining the Plan

## Slide 1 — Week 11
### Reviewing and Refining the Plan
Quality assurance before implementation.

---
## Slide 2 — Planning chain
**Requirements → audience/objectives → platform/content → publication → community/policy → measures**

---
## Slide 3 — Review cycle
**Plan → test → feedback → analyse → refine/retain → justify → implementation baseline**

---
## Slide 4 — Review ≠ proofreading
Spelling matters, but B6 review asks whether the campaign plan will actually meet requirements.

---
## Slide 5 — Four review lenses
Requirements • coherence • feasibility • measurability.

---
## Slide 6 — Requirements lens
Can every major planning decision be traced back to a business need/objective?

---
## Slide 7 — Coherence lens
Do audience, platform, content, timing, interaction policy and measures support each other?

---
## Slide 8 — Feasibility lens
Can the plan be delivered with the available time, people, content-production capability and simulator features?

---
## Slide 9 — Measurability lens
Will the chosen measures actually tell you whether objectives were achieved?

---
## Slide 10 — Audit for gaps
What requirement has no activity? What activity has no purpose? What objective has no measure?

---
## Slide 11 — Audit contradictions
Examples: encouraging conversation but no response resource; targeting click-through but measuring only likes.

---
## Slide 12 — Stakeholder feedback
Feedback should provide another informed perspective on how effectively the plan may meet its purpose.

---
## Slide 13 — Ask useful questions
“Do you like it?” produces preference. Ask focused questions tied to requirements and intended outcomes.

---
## Slide 14 — Stakeholder relevance
Consider why this stakeholder's perspective is useful — and what it cannot tell you.

---
## Slide 15 — Record accurately
Do not rewrite feedback afterwards to make your decision look stronger.

---
## Slide 16 — Feedback is not an instruction
You remain responsible for deciding whether a suggestion improves the plan.

---
## Slide 17 — Accept, adapt or reject
All three can be valid when justified.

---
## Slide 18 — Prioritisation
Consider **impact × validity/evidence × feasibility**.

---
## Slide 19 — High-impact feedback
Feedback exposing failure to meet a key requirement should normally receive more attention than a minor aesthetic preference.

---
## Slide 20 — Conflicting feedback
Two stakeholders may disagree. Return to requirements, audience evidence and constraints.

---
## Slide 21 — Refinement log
Record original issue/decision, feedback, action, rationale and effect on the wider plan.

---
## Slide 22 — Change dependencies
Changing one element may require updates elsewhere: platform → format → schedule → measures → policy/resources.

---
## Slide 23 — Retaining is a decision
“No change” can be a strong outcome if the original choice remains better supported.

---
## Slide 24 — Weak justification
“I changed the post because my peer said it was boring.”

---
## Slide 25 — Stronger reasoning
Explain the issue, requirement, alternatives, likely consequences and why the final decision is preferable.

---
## Slide 26 — B.P4
Demonstrate that the plan has been reviewed and improved/refined using appropriate feedback.

---
## Slide 27 — B.M2
Justify the significant planning decisions and refinements.

---
## Slide 28 — Towards BC.D3
Develop evaluative judgement across the campaign process. Do not claim implementation success before implementation evidence exists.

---
## Slide 29 — DojoLearn configuration
The simulator should reflect the refined plan, not an earlier draft.

---
## Slide 30 — Configuration check
Platforms • campaign targets • available settings • content readiness • responsibilities/processes • baseline evidence.

---
## Slide 31 — Simulator limitations
Record limitations. Do not invent features to make the plan appear complete.

---
## Slide 32 — Freeze the baseline
Capture the final pre-implementation state so later evidence can be compared with what was actually planned.

---
## Slide 33 — Implementation readiness
Can another person understand what should happen, why, when, who is responsible and how success will be judged?

---
## Slide 34 — Next phase
### Implementation
The question changes from **“What should we do?”** to **“What happened when we did it?”**
