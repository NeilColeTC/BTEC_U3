# Week 3 Slides — Audience Profiles and Targeting

## Slide 1 — Week 3
### Audience Profiles and Targeting
Last week: **what can a business do?** Today: **who is it trying to reach?**

---
## Slide 2 — Retrieval
Name four business uses of social media. Why should a business identify its objective before choosing a platform?

---
## Slide 3 — A.P2 direction
A.P2 requires learners to **explain the audience profiles of different social-media websites**.

---
## Slide 4 — Target audience
The group of people a business intends its communication, product or service to reach.

---
## Slide 5 — Audience profile
Characteristics used to describe an audience or audience segment. Unit 3 specifically directs attention to **age, gender, interests and income**.

---
## Slide 6 — Age
Age ranges can be useful when evidence shows differences in platform use, needs or communication preferences. Avoid assuming everyone of the same age behaves identically.

---
## Slide 7 — Gender
Gender may form part of audience data. Treat patterns as evidence to interpret, not stereotypes to apply to individuals.

---
## Slide 8 — Interests
Interests can be especially useful when a business is trying to reach communities connected to products, activities, hobbies, causes or topics.

---
## Slide 9 — Income
Income may affect purchasing power or relevance for some products/services. It should be used carefully and only when relevant to the business decision.

---
## Slide 10 — Profiles are combinations
A useful audience profile may combine characteristics. Example structure: age range + relevant interests + purchasing/context factors + platform behaviour evidence.

---
## Slide 11 — Avoid stereotypes
“Teenagers use…” is not evidence. Ask: **What does the data show? Who collected it? When? In what context?**

---
## Slide 12 — Platforms attract different audiences
Differences can relate to platform purpose, features, content formats, communities and patterns of adoption/use.

---
## Slide 13 — Evidence matters
Use **claim → source/evidence → interpretation → business significance**.

---
## Slide 14 — Data changes
Social-media audiences evolve. Audience research should be sufficiently current for the decision being made.

---
## Slide 15 — Reading audience data
When reading a chart/table ask: What population is measured? Percentage of what? What is the sample/context? What conclusion is justified?

---
## Slide 16 — Audience overlap
Platforms are not isolated demographic boxes. The same person can use multiple platforms for different purposes.

---
## Slide 17 — DojoLearn persona challenge
Given a business objective and persona, recommend a simulated platform. Justify using platform characteristics and audience needs.

---
## Slide 18 — What evidence is missing?
DojoLearn lets us practise reasoning, but a real recommendation would require reliable current evidence about actual platform audiences.

---
## Slide 19 — A.P2 explanation structure
**profile characteristic → evidence → what it suggests about the platform audience → why that matters**.

---
## Slide 20 — Compare, don't just list
Platform A and Platform B may overlap but differ in important ways. Explain those differences rather than producing two disconnected lists.

---
## Slide 21 — Exit ticket
Define audience profile; explain one characteristic; explain one platform difference; identify one reason evidence matters.
