# Week 3 Key Terminology

| Term | Working definition | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Target audience | The group a business intends to reach. | Business communication and platform choices should relate to intended recipients. |
| Audience profile | A description of characteristics associated with an audience or audience segment. | A.P2 requires explanation of audience profiles of different social-media websites. |
| Demographic | A measurable characteristic used to describe groups within a population. | Age, gender and income can contribute to audience analysis. |
| Age profile | Information about the ages/age ranges represented within an audience. | Can help identify patterns in platform audiences when supported by evidence. |
| Gender profile | Information about gender representation within an audience. | May be relevant to audience analysis but should not be converted into stereotypes. |
| Interest | Topic, activity or subject an audience is interested in. | Interests can connect business content with relevant communities. |
| Income | Financial-income information associated with an audience/segment. | May be relevant to purchasing context for some business decisions. |
| Persona | A fictional representation of a target customer/audience member based on relevant information. | Helps learners practise connecting audience needs to platform/content decisions. |
| Audience segment | A subgroup of a wider audience sharing selected characteristics. | Businesses may need different approaches for different parts of an audience. |
| Evidence | Information/data supporting a claim. | Audience claims should be researched rather than assumed. |
| Source | Where information/evidence originates. | Currency and reliability affect the usefulness of audience research. |
| Audience overlap | The presence of the same or similar users across more than one platform. | Prevents simplistic assumptions that platforms have completely separate audiences. |
| Targeting | Directing communication towards an intended audience/segment. | Links audience understanding to later business-use and campaign decisions. |

These are teaching definitions rather than assignment-ready responses.
