# Week 3 Lesson Plan — Audience Profiles and Targeting

**Duration:** 3 hours  
**Learning Aim:** A  
**Specification:** A1/A2  
**Primary criterion preparation:** A.P2

## Learning outcomes

Learners will define audience profiles, investigate age/gender/interests/income, explain differences between platform audiences and justify platform choices for audience personas.

## Lesson sequence

### 1. Retrieval — 15 minutes

Retrieve Week 2 business uses and the principle **objective before platform**. Include one bridging question: why does a business need to know who it is trying to reach?

### 2. Target audience vs audience profile — 20 minutes

Teach the distinction:

- **target audience:** group the business intends to reach;
- **audience profile:** characteristics used to describe that group or the users of a platform.

Introduce the specification characteristics: **age, gender, interests and income**.

### 3. Building useful audience profiles — 30 minutes

Learners complete the audience-profile builder. Discuss why vague labels such as “young people” are weak and why characteristics need context.

Explicitly challenge stereotypes. A profile describes patterns/segments; it does not prove that every person within a demographic behaves identically.

### 4. Platform audience investigation — 35 minutes

Using current research/data sources selected by the teacher, learners compare audience profiles for different social-media websites.

For each claim require:

**claim → evidence/source → interpretation → business significance**.

Discuss source currency, sample/context and the difference between evidence and assumption.

### Break — 10 minutes

### 5. DojoLearn audience matching — 35 minutes

Learners receive audience personas and business objectives. They choose suitable simulated platforms based on platform characteristics explored in Weeks 1–2 and justify the match.

Require learners to identify what further audience evidence would be needed before making a real business decision.

### 6. A.P2 formative practice — 35 minutes

Learners explain the audience profiles of at least two social-media website types/platforms using teacher-provided practice evidence. They compare the profiles and explain why the differences could matter to a business.

This is formative and should not use the exact evidence/examples intended for the live assignment.

### 7. Exit ticket — 10 minutes

Independent check on audience profile, evidence, platform difference and business relevance.

## Adaptive teaching

**Support:** profile templates, vocabulary prompts, pre-selected data extracts and sentence stems.  
**Stretch:** critique evidence quality, identify overlap between platform audiences, distinguish correlation from certainty and explain why multi-platform strategies may be useful.

## English, maths, EDI and employability

**English:** interpreting source material and writing evidence-supported explanations.  
**Maths:** reading percentages, proportions, demographic charts and comparing distributions where available.  
**EDI:** explicit avoidance of demographic stereotyping; recognise diversity within audience segments.  
**Employability:** customer segmentation, research literacy, evidence-based marketing decisions and source evaluation.

## Teacher reflection

- Are learners describing profiles or stereotyping individuals?
- Can they distinguish evidence from assumption?
- Can they explain why an audience characteristic matters to a business?
- Are A.P2 responses genuinely explanatory?
- Which learners are ready to combine A1 and A2 reasoning in Week 4?
