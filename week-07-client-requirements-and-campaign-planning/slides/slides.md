# Week 7 Slides — Client Requirements and Campaign Planning

## Slide 1 — Week 7
### Client Requirements and Campaign Planning
Learning Aim B begins: **plan before you publish**.

---
## Slide 2 — From A to B
Learning Aim A asked how businesses use social media. Learning Aim B asks how a campaign should be planned to meet requirements.

---
## Slide 3 — Planning chain
**Requirement → objective → activity → measure**

---
## Slide 4 — Start with the brief
A campaign is not “make some social posts”. It is a coordinated response to a business need, audience and set of requirements.

---
## Slide 5 — Business/client requirement
Something the campaign needs to achieve, include, respect or work within because of the business/client context.

---
## Slide 6 — Explicit vs implied
**Explicit:** directly stated.  
**Implied:** appears necessary from the context but may require clarification. Do not silently invent client requirements.

---
## Slide 7 — Campaign purpose
Why does the campaign exist? Examples of purpose categories may include awareness, communication, engagement, traffic or encouraging a defined action — but the actual purpose must come from the scenario.

---
## Slide 8 — Target audience
Who must the campaign reach or influence? Reuse Learning Aim A evidence skills: audience decisions should not rely on stereotypes.

---
## Slide 9 — Aim vs objective
**Aim:** broad intended direction/outcome.  
**Objective:** more specific result against which progress can be judged.

---
## Slide 10 — Weak objective
“Get more engagement.” What does **more** mean? By when? Which relevant interaction? Why does it matter?

---
## Slide 11 — Measurable does not mean random
Adding an arbitrary percentage does not automatically create a good objective. The measure and target should be meaningful for the requirement and available evidence.

---
## Slide 12 — SMART quality check
Specific • Measurable • Achievable • Relevant • Time-related. Use it to test clarity, not as a substitute for business reasoning.

---
## Slide 13 — Success criteria
What evidence would indicate that the campaign has met its objectives/requirements?

---
## Slide 14 — Baseline
A starting value/state allows later performance to be compared with where the campaign began.

---
## Slide 15 — Measures
Possible measures depend on objectives: reach, engagement, clicks/actions, enquiries, conversions or other simulator/business evidence. Choose the measure because it answers a question.

---
## Slide 16 — Avoid vanity metrics
A large number is not automatically useful. Ask whether the measure demonstrates progress towards the campaign objective.

---
## Slide 17 — Timescale
Campaign duration, milestones, deadlines and frequency expectations affect what can realistically be planned and measured.

---
## Slide 18 — Responsibilities
Who is responsible for planning, creating, checking, publishing, monitoring or responding? Responsibility should be clear enough for the campaign to be manageable.

---
## Slide 19 — Constraints
Time, resources, platform capability, brand expectations, permissions and other requirements can limit available choices.

---
## Slide 20 — Traceability
For every major decision ask: **Which requirement/objective does this support?**

---
## Slide 21 — DojoLearn baseline
Establish the campaign/business context and record the starting state before implementation.

---
## Slide 22 — Do not publish yet
Week 7 establishes requirements and objectives. Platform/content strategy develops next.

---
## Slide 23 — B.P3 direction
A campaign plan must be sufficiently clear that another person could understand what is intended and how it relates to requirements.

---
## Slide 24 — Quality test
Are requirements identified? Objectives measurable? Audience clear? Timescale realistic? Responsibilities identified? Measures meaningful?

---
## Slide 25 — Exit ticket
Requirement → objective → measure. Can you show the connection?
