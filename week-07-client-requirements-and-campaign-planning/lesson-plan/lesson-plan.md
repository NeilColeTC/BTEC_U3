# Week 7 Lesson Plan — Client Requirements and Campaign Planning

**Duration:** 3 hours  
**Learning Aim:** B  
**Specification:** B1/B2  
**Primary criterion preparation:** B.P3

## Learning outcomes

Learners will interpret business/client requirements, construct measurable campaign objectives and establish a planning baseline for a social-media campaign.

## Lesson sequence

### 1. Retrieval and transition from Learning Aim A — 15 minutes

Retrieve target audience, business objective, reach, engagement, business outcome, platform suitability and risk. Ask learners to distinguish **analysing an existing approach** from **planning what should happen next**.

### 2. Assignment 2 / Learning Aim B orientation — 15 minutes

Introduce the planning phase and B.P3 direction. Clarify that the campaign should be designed around requirements rather than around whichever platform/content learners personally prefer.

### 3. Deconstructing a client/business brief — 30 minutes

Using a neutral formative brief, identify:

- business need/problem/opportunity;
- campaign purpose;
- intended target audience;
- explicit requirements;
- implied requirements requiring clarification;
- constraints;
- timescale;
- responsibilities;
- evidence of success.

Learners complete the client-brief deconstruction sheet.

### 4. From aims to measurable objectives — 30 minutes

Teach the distinction between broad aims and measurable campaign objectives. Introduce a SMART-style quality check without encouraging meaningless precision.

Learners improve weak objectives using the objective builder.

### 5. Campaign requirements grid — 20 minutes

Learners connect each requirement to a planned response and a possible measure. Emphasise traceability: every major campaign decision should eventually be defensible against a requirement/objective.

### Break — 10 minutes

### 6. DojoLearn campaign setup/baseline — 35 minutes

Learners establish the simulated business/campaign workspace as directed. They record:

- campaign/business identity;
- intended purpose;
- target audience;
- starting/baseline state visible in the simulator;
- available platform capabilities relevant to later planning;
- any constraints or setup decisions.

Do not publish campaign content yet unless the supplied simulator workflow requires a harmless setup action.

### 7. B.P3 formative planning practice — 25 minutes

Learners independently respond to a neutral campaign scenario by identifying requirements, objectives, measures, timescale and responsibilities. Peer review focuses on completeness and traceability rather than supplying better answers.

### 8. Exit ticket — 15 minutes

Learners distinguish aims/objectives/requirements/measures and improve one weak objective.

## Adaptive teaching

**Support:** colour-code client briefs, requirement categories, objective sentence frames and worked non-assessed examples.  
**Stretch:** identify conflicting requirements, assumptions needing clarification, dependencies and measures that could create misleading conclusions.

## English, maths, EDI and employability

**English:** interpreting client language and writing precise objectives.  
**Maths:** measurable targets, timescales, baseline values and percentage/number-based success measures where appropriate.  
**EDI:** campaign planning should avoid stereotypical audience assumptions and consider accessible communication needs from the outset.  
**Employability:** client requirements analysis, project planning, objective setting, responsibility allocation and evidence-based decision making.

## Teacher reflection

- Can learners distinguish aims from measurable objectives?
- Are targets connected to business requirements?
- Are learners inventing requirements unsupported by the brief?
- Do proposed measures actually indicate progress towards the objective?
- Are learners planning before creating?
