# Week 5 Teacher Notes

## Curriculum intent

Week 5 completes the substantive Learning Aim A teaching sequence before the Week 6 assessment workshop. It introduces A3 risks/issues while requiring learners to synthesise Weeks 2–4 in preparation for A.D1.

## Specification fidelity

The core A3 areas in the repository specification map are:

- negative comments and reputational damage;
- time constraints and return on time investment;
- unforeseen consequences of content;
- security threats;
- loss of sensitive information.

The existing Week 5 scaffold also identifies legal and ethical implications. Use these to contextualise responsible practice, while keeping the principal focus on the A3 risks/issues and A.D1 evaluation.

## Teaching evaluation

Learners should not be taught that distinction is achieved by writing a longer Merit response. Evaluation requires weighing significance.

Useful questions:

- Which benefit matters most to this business and audience?
- Which risk has the greatest potential consequence?
- How likely is it?
- Can it be mitigated?
- Does mitigation change the overall judgement?
- Under what conditions would the strategy be worthwhile?

## Complaints and moderation

Avoid simplistic rules such as “always reply immediately” or “delete negative comments”. Learners should classify the situation and choose a proportionate response. Genuine complaints, abusive content, misinformation, spam and security incidents may require different actions.

## Public/private boundary

Emphasise that public responses should not reveal customer details. Moving a case to a private channel does not remove the need for professional handling or appropriate escalation.

## Legal/ethical teaching

Keep statements appropriately cautious. Learners should recognise areas such as privacy/data protection, copyright/permissions, confidentiality, misleading communication, discrimination and respectful conduct. Detailed legal interpretation is outside the purpose of this lesson unless supported by current authoritative material.

## DojoLearn

Use the simulator to make reputation management observable and repeatable. Require a decision log so practical clicking does not replace reasoning. Where the simulator provides consequences or analytics, learners should record them as evidence rather than inventing outcomes.

## Adaptive teaching

### Support
- classify scenarios before writing responses;
- use risk/consequence/control sentence stems;
- provide professional-response prompts;
- orally rehearse difficult responses;
- use a partially completed evaluation model.

### Stretch
- prioritise competing risks;
- consider multiple stakeholders;
- distinguish likelihood and impact;
- propose preventive and reactive controls;
- write conditional conclusions.

## EDI and professional conduct

Do not assume that the person making a complaint is unreasonable. Discuss respectful communication, accessibility and how language/tone can affect different users. Professionalism applies even where criticism is uncomfortable.

## Assessment boundary

All practice scenarios are deliberately separate from the live assignment. Teacher feedback should focus on reasoning quality and command-term understanding rather than supplying content for the assessed response.

## Preparation for Week 6

Week 6 should consolidate A.P1, A.P2, A.M1 and A.D1, audit evidence requirements, practise planning/research and provide structured opportunities for independent assignment work without producing the learner's assessed answer.
