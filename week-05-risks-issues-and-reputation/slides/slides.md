# Week 5 Slides — Risks, Issues and Reputation

## Slide 1 — Week 5
### Risks, Issues and Reputation
Social media creates opportunity — and exposure.

---
## Slide 2 — Retrieval
What are reach, engagement and business outcome? What does **assess** require? Give one attraction technique and one possible limitation.

---
## Slide 3 — A.D1 direction
A.D1 requires evaluation of business use of social media to **interact with customers** and **promote products/services** to a **target audience**.

---
## Slide 4 — Assess vs evaluate
Evaluation weighs significance and competing factors to reach a reasoned overall conclusion. It is not a longer list of pros and cons.

---
## Slide 5 — Opportunity and risk
The ability to communicate quickly, publicly and directly creates value — but those same characteristics can amplify mistakes and criticism.

---
## Slide 6 — Negative comments
Customers can publicly express dissatisfaction. Consider visibility, speed of spread, permanence/screenshots and the business response.

---
## Slide 7 — Reputation
Reputation is shaped not only by what happens, but by how the business responds. Other users may observe the interaction.

---
## Slide 8 — Not every negative comment is the same
Distinguish genuine complaint, question, criticism, misinformation, spam and abusive content. Response should be proportionate.

---
## Slide 9 — Time constraints
Publishing, monitoring, responding and analysing all require staff time. Ask whether the activity produces sufficient business value for the resources used.

---
## Slide 10 — Return on time
High activity is not automatically productive. What useful outcome does the work support?

---
## Slide 11 — Unforeseen consequences
Content may be misunderstood, shared outside its intended context, become outdated, offend an audience or reveal more than intended.

---
## Slide 12 — Think before publishing
Audience + accuracy + tone + timing + permissions + confidentiality + accessibility + likely interpretation.

---
## Slide 13 — Security threats
Possible issues include account compromise, phishing/social engineering, weak credentials, malicious links and unauthorised access.

---
## Slide 14 — Information loss/exposure
Businesses should protect personal, commercially sensitive and confidential information. Public posting can make errors difficult to reverse.

---
## Slide 15 — Legal and ethical context
Responsible businesses should consider privacy/data protection, copyright/permissions, misleading claims, confidentiality, discrimination and respectful communication where relevant.

---
## Slide 16 — Risk thinking
**Likelihood** asks how plausible an event is. **Impact** asks how serious the consequence could be. High impact deserves attention even if likelihood is lower.

---
## Slide 17 — Controls
Possible controls include account security, permissions, review/approval, staff guidance, monitoring, escalation routes and clear response procedures.

---
## Slide 18 — Complaints are also interactions
A complaint can damage reputation, but a professional response can demonstrate customer service to the wider audience.

---
## Slide 19 — DojoLearn practical
Classify → decide → respond → escalate if needed → record reasoning → consider wider audience.

---
## Slide 20 — Professional response principles
Acknowledge appropriately. Stay factual. Avoid arguments. Protect private information. Move sensitive detail away from public view. Escalate when required.

---
## Slide 21 — Evaluation model
**Benefit → evidence/context → risk/limitation → significance → mitigation → overall judgement.**

---
## Slide 22 — Avoid extremes
“Social media is dangerous” is weak. “Social media is always essential” is also weak. Evaluation depends on business, audience, management and purpose.

---
## Slide 23 — A.D1 practice
Evaluate whether social media is an effective way for a fictional business to interact with customers and promote its services.

---
## Slide 24 — Overall conclusion
Which benefits matter most? Which risks matter most? Can they be managed? What conditions make social media worthwhile?

---
## Slide 25 — Exit ticket
Risk → consequence → response → judgement.
