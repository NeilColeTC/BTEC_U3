# Week 5 — Risks, Issues and Reputation

**Specification focus:** Learning Aim A — A3 Risks and issues  
**Assessment preparation:** A.D1  
**Practical environment:** DojoLearn social-media simulation

## Week purpose

Week 5 develops the balanced evaluative thinking required for A.D1. Learners explore how the same social-media features that allow businesses to interact with customers and promote products/services can also create reputational, operational and security risks.

The key idea is that effective business social-media use requires **opportunity + risk + response + judgement**.

## Learning outcomes

Learners should be able to:

1. Explain how negative comments can affect a business and its reputation.
2. Explain why managing social media creates time/resource demands.
3. Analyse unforeseen consequences of inappropriate, inaccurate or poorly judged content.
4. Explain security threats and possible loss of sensitive/personal information.
5. Consider relevant legal and ethical implications when managing content and customer interaction.
6. Evaluate business use of social media for interaction and promotion by weighing benefits against risks and reaching a supported conclusion.
7. Respond professionally to simulated complaints and reputation scenarios in DojoLearn.

## A3 focus

- negative comments and reputational damage;
- time constraints and return on time investment;
- unforeseen consequences of content;
- security threats;
- loss of sensitive/personal information.

Legal and ethical implications are used as supporting context where relevant to responsible business decisions.

## Learning progression

**identify risk → explain consequence → consider likelihood/impact → propose response → weigh against benefit → evaluate**

## DojoLearn practical

Learners receive simulated customer comments and reputation-management incidents. They decide whether/how to respond, draft professional responses, explain escalation decisions and evaluate how their actions may affect both the individual customer and the wider observing audience.
