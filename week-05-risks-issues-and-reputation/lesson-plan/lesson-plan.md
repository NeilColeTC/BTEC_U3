# Week 5 Lesson Plan — Risks, Issues and Reputation

**Duration:** 3 hours  
**Learning Aim:** A  
**Specification:** A3  
**Primary criterion preparation:** A.D1

## Learning outcomes

Learners will analyse risks/issues created by business social-media use, practise professional response decisions and evaluate benefits and drawbacks in context.

## Lesson sequence

### 1. Retrieval and bridge from Week 4 — 15 minutes

Retrieve target audience, attraction, engagement, reach, business outcome and assessment/judgement. Ask: **How can a feature that creates an opportunity also create a risk?**

### 2. From assess to evaluate — 20 minutes

Model progression:

- explain: how/why;
- assess: compare relevant factors and reach a judgement;
- evaluate: weigh significance, consequences and competing factors to reach a reasoned conclusion about overall value/effectiveness.

Stress that evaluation is not simply adding more disadvantages.

### 3. A3 risks and issues — 35 minutes

Teach:

- negative comments and reputation;
- time/resource constraints and return on time;
- unforeseen consequences of content;
- security threats;
- loss of sensitive/personal information.

Learners complete a risk-impact matrix.

### 4. Content consequences, legal and ethical context — 30 minutes

Use short fictional cases involving inaccurate posts, inappropriate humour, unauthorised information sharing, poor handling of customer data and account compromise.

Learners identify stakeholders, possible consequences and responsible action. Legal/ethical points should support the analysis without turning the lesson into specialist legal training.

### Break — 10 minutes

### 5. DojoLearn reputation-management practical — 45 minutes

Learners respond to simulated comments/incidents. Require them to:

- classify the issue;
- decide whether a public response is appropriate;
- draft a professional response;
- decide whether escalation/private handling is needed;
- explain the reputational implications;
- record evidence of their decision.

### 6. A.D1 formative evaluation — 35 minutes

Using a neutral fictional business, learners evaluate social media as a method for customer interaction and promotion to a target audience. They must balance benefits from Weeks 2–4 with A3 risks and reach an overall supported conclusion.

### 7. Exit ticket — 10 minutes

Independent risk/consequence/response question and short evaluative judgement.

## Adaptive teaching

**Support:** risk categories, consequence prompts, response sentence stems and worked evaluation paragraph.  
**Stretch:** prioritise risks, distinguish likelihood from impact, identify stakeholder conflicts, propose proportionate controls and write conditional conclusions.

## English, maths, EDI and employability

**English:** professional customer-response writing and evaluative connectives.  
**Maths:** simple likelihood/impact scoring and prioritisation.  
**EDI:** consider how tone, language, humour and inaccessible/inappropriate content may affect different audiences; avoid stereotyping complainants.  
**Employability:** reputation management, customer service, escalation, information security and professional judgement.

## Teacher reflection

- Do learners distinguish criticism from abuse/security incidents?
- Are responses proportionate rather than defensive?
- Can learners explain why public interactions affect observing audiences?
- Do evaluations weigh opportunity and risk rather than merely list both?
