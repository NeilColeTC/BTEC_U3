# Week 15 — Content Optimisation

**Specification focus:** Learning Aim C — C2/C4  
**Assessment preparation:** C.M3  
**Practical environment:** DojoLearn social-media simulation

## Week purpose

Week 15 closes the loop between analytics and implementation. Learners use Week 14 evidence to identify a justified optimisation opportunity, make a controlled change to campaign content or activity, capture before/after evidence and compare the resulting performance where sufficient data is available.

The central optimisation cycle is:

**evidence → problem/opportunity → hypothesis → controlled change → implement → measure → compare → judgement**

## Learning outcomes

Learners should be able to:

1. Select optimisation priorities from analytics evidence rather than personal preference.
2. Distinguish optimisation from arbitrary redesign.
3. Form an evidence-based hypothesis about a change and expected effect.
4. Identify the campaign variable being changed and relevant comparison measure.
5. Improve content, format, timing or platform-feature use where supported by evidence.
6. Maintain campaign requirements, branding, policy and audience appropriateness during optimisation.
7. Capture clear before/after evidence.
8. Compare post-change performance cautiously and fairly.
9. Document what changed, why and what the evidence suggests.
10. Build evidence towards C.M3 and the Week 16 BC.D3 campaign evaluation.

## C2/C4 focus

This week uses analytics to improve live campaign implementation and evaluate the effect of evidence-based changes.

## DojoLearn practical

Learners identify one or more justified optimisation opportunities, preserve the original evidence, implement controlled changes within the simulator and gather comparison evidence without inventing results where insufficient time/data exists.

## Assessment boundary

Teachers may teach optimisation methods and provide neutral examples, but should not choose the learner's optimisation, rewrite assessed campaign content or supply the learner's final judgement about whether a change was effective.
