# Week 15 Slides — Content Optimisation

## Slide 1 — Week 15
### Content Optimisation
Change because the evidence gives you a reason.

---
## Slide 2 — C2/C4
Use analytics to improve campaign content/activity and evaluate the effect of the change.

---
## Slide 3 — Optimisation cycle
**Evidence → problem/opportunity → hypothesis → controlled change → implement → measure → compare → judgement**

---
## Slide 4 — Optimisation ≠ redesign
Changing something is not automatically improving it.

---
## Slide 5 — Weak reason
“I changed the image because the new one looks better.”

---
## Slide 6 — Stronger reason
“Analytics identified weaker interaction/action against comparable content, so I changed one relevant feature and monitored the same outcome.”

---
## Slide 7 — Start with Week 14
Which finding identifies a meaningful problem or opportunity?

---
## Slide 8 — Optimisation targets
Content/message • format • CTA • timing/frequency • visibility technique • platform emphasis • available campaign feature.

---
## Slide 9 — Prioritise
Consider **objective relevance • evidence strength • likely impact • feasibility • measurability**.

---
## Slide 10 — Hypothesis
State what you will change, what you expect may happen and why.

---
## Slide 11 — Hypothesis stem
**Because [evidence], changing [variable] from [before] to [after] may improve [measure/outcome] because [reason].**

---
## Slide 12 — Variable
The specific feature/factor deliberately changed.

---
## Slide 13 — Controlled change
Where possible, avoid changing several major factors at once if you want to learn what may have influenced the result.

---
## Slide 14 — Confounding variable
Another changing factor that could explain part/all of the observed difference.

---
## Slide 15 — Before evidence
Capture the original state **before** editing or replacing it.

---
## Slide 16 — Define the measure
What metric/outcome will indicate whether the change appears useful?

---
## Slide 17 — Define the comparison
What will the revised activity be compared with?

---
## Slide 18 — Fairness
Consider exposure, time live, platform, audience opportunity, campaign stage and purpose.

---
## Slide 19 — DojoLearn
Use only features/data the simulation actually provides.

---
## Slide 20 — Content/message optimisation
Improve clarity, relevance, value or audience alignment when evidence supports it.

---
## Slide 21 — CTA optimisation
If the objective depends on action, investigate whether the requested next step is clear and appropriate.

---
## Slide 22 — Format optimisation
Change format only when there is a campaign/evidence reason — not because one format is fashionable.

---
## Slide 23 — Timing/frequency
Performance differences may justify testing timing/frequency, but other variables can also explain those differences.

---
## Slide 24 — Visibility techniques
Refine hashtags, tags, sharing/following or other available discovery features where evidence supports it.

---
## Slide 25 — Platform emphasis
Analytics may suggest a different allocation of effort, but compare platform metrics cautiously.

---
## Slide 26 — Protect requirements
Optimisation must not abandon business requirements or campaign objectives simply to increase a metric.

---
## Slide 27 — Protect the audience
A higher number is not an improvement if the change makes content less appropriate or accessible to the intended audience.

---
## Slide 28 — Protect policy/brand
Optimisation remains subject to social-media policy, professional identity and brand consistency.

---
## Slide 29 — Implement and record
Update implementation/deviation evidence so the campaign history remains traceable.

---
## Slide 30 — After evidence
Capture the same relevant metric(s) where possible after the change.

---
## Slide 31 — Compare
Use raw difference, rate or percentage change only when the comparison is mathematically and contextually valid.

---
## Slide 32 — Interpretation
Did the relevant outcome improve, worsen or remain inconclusive?

---
## Slide 33 — Do not overclaim
Improvement after a change does not automatically prove the change caused it.

---
## Slide 34 — Decision options
**Retain • refine further • revert • insufficient evidence yet**.

---
## Slide 35 — C.M3
Show how campaign data was used to optimise implementation and analyse the effect of those improvements.

---
## Slide 36 — Reasoning structure
**Change → before evidence → after evidence → comparison → interpretation → limitation → decision**

---
## Slide 37 — Evidence chain
Week 14 finding → Week 15 decision → implementation evidence → post-change evidence → judgement.

---
## Slide 38 — Failure can be useful
An optimisation that does not improve performance can still demonstrate strong analytical practice if the process and judgement are evidence-based.

---
## Slide 39 — Insufficient evidence is valid
Do not manufacture a success claim because the observation period is short.

---
## Slide 40 — Week 16 bridge
The final evaluation can now examine planning decisions, implementation, analytics, optimisation, limitations and overall business impact.
